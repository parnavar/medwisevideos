1. Structural Morphology Profile

Feature Area Anatomical Description & Landmark Elements
3 Borders Superior: Shortest/thinnest border. Medial (Vertebral): Longest border, mirrors the spine line. Lateral (Axillary): Thickest border, points toward the armpit.
3 Angles Superior Angle: Where superior and medial borders converge. Inferior Angle: Lowest convergence tip; moves during arm rotation. Lateral Angle: Broad head containing the glenoid articulation socket.
2 Surfaces Costal (Anterior): Concave subscapular fossa sits directly against the posterior ribs. Dorsal (Posterior): Divided by the prominent spine into supraspinous and infraspinous fossae.

2. Clinical Pathology Reference

🪽 Winging of Scapula

Caused by injury to the Long Thoracic Nerve paralyzing the Serratus Anterior. The medial border of the scapula protrudes backward like a wing when the patient pushes against a wall.

💥 Impingement Syndrome

Compression of the Supraspinatus tendon inside the narrow gap under the acromion process, causing severe pain during overhead arm movements.
